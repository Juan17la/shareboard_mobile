Objective

Let anyone go from nothing to drawing together in seconds: open the app, draw, press Share, read out six characters.

Most collaborative boards ask for an account, a workspace and a tour before the first stroke. In a classroom, a study group or a quick call that cost is bigger than the drawing itself. Shareboard removes it: the app opens already on a board that works offline, and sharing turns it into a live board everyone can join from a phone or a browser.

0
steps before the first stroke

No home page, no sign-up, no workspace. The board is the first screen.

6
characters to share

A code without look-alikes, easy to say aloud or write on a whiteboard: K7M·Q2R.

2
platforms, one mental model

Web and Android with the same tools in the same places, drawing on the same board live.

Goals

For users
  • Sketch, diagram and brainstorm with others in real time, on whatever device is at hand.
  • Never lose work: an offline board that is always there, and reconnection that keeps strokes safe.
  • Control who sees and who edits, without learning a permissions system.
  • Take results anywhere: images that can be re-opened as editable boards.
For the project
  • Demonstrate interface design decisions measured against a clear success criterion.
  • Keep web and Android at feature parity with a shared visual language.
  • Stay light to run: one server, optional database, free AI providers.

Target audience

Primary: students and class groups who sketch, diagram and brainstorm together, mostly on Android phones and laptops, in sessions of a few minutes started from a shared code. Secondary: teachers and facilitators who run activities, and developers who draw quick diagrams. Ages range from children to older teachers, so nothing may depend on jargon or prior tool knowledge.

The style: "The Metro Map"

Shareboard is drawn as a small transit system. The board is the city; everything around it is signage. Each place in the app is a station, each flow is a line, and the share code is a line badge you read out loud. The interface is solid, flat and high-contrast, like enamel signs.

Wayfinding, not decoration

The style borrows from transit signage because its job is the same: tell people where they are and where to go, at a glance, for anyone. Menus are drawn as lines with stations; multi-step flows as stops.

Colour with a job

Four saturated lines, one meaning each: blue acts and selects, green finishes, orange warns, violet marks app-wide places. A screen shows only the lines it needs.

Solid and readable

Opaque white panels, signage ink text, 2 px rings on tappable circles, pill buttons. No glass, no blur: text stays readable over any drawing.

Compact and quiet

Chrome stays small and out of the way so the drawing gets the space. Rarely used actions hide in the menu; the options strip shows only what the tool in hand needs.

Two worlds

The UI speaks Onest and JetBrains Mono in signage colours. The board speaks its own language: friendly fonts (Nunito, Lora, Caveat) and a vivid drawing palette. They never mix.

Friendly, not childish

Animal identity icons, sticky notes and handwriting on the board keep it warm; the chrome stays sober so teachers and developers take it seriously.

Product principles

1 · Be drawing in seconds

Nothing blocks the first stroke: no home, no login, no tour unless asked.

2 · Ask only when it unlocks something

An account is requested at the moment of sharing, and the app says why.

3 · Every menu is reachable, dismissable and predictable

By touch and by keyboard, with the same close gestures everywhere.

4 · One mental model across platforms

Web and Android put the same things in the same places with the same names.

Voice and tone

Short, plain, warm. Talk to a person, not a user. Say what happened and what to do next. Bilingual: Spanish first, English equal.

How we measure success

Primary

A first-time user draws, then shares a board, without help.

Time to first stroke

Under 2 seconds from launch, even offline.

Time to join

A second person joins from a spoken code in under 30 seconds.

Parity

Every feature exists on web and Android with the same name and place.

Comprehension

In usability tests, participants find Share, Access and Export without hints.

Accessibility

44 px touch targets, AA contrast in both themes, full keyboard use on web.

No usage analytics are collected today; these are checked through moderated tests and the performance kit.
